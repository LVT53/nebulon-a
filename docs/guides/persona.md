# Phase 1 - Persona

**Maya, 20.** Second-year creative media student at TUS. Space is a hobby, not a subject. She saw the Webb images when they came out, watched a launch video once because it was genuinely tense, and has fallen down a black hole YouTube spiral at 1am. She could not explain what an exoplanet transit is, but she wants to know. Maya is a composite of people I actually know who fit this: interested, but not a specialist.

## How she uses a site like this

Phone, one-handed, between lectures or in her room at night, in windows of five to ten minutes. She decides within seconds whether a page is for her: title, first line, glance at the image. If the first paragraph uses jargon with no explanation, she leaves.

## What she wants

- To understand one thing properly rather than skim ten headlines.
- To know before she taps how long a read takes and whether it will lose her.
- Something she can act on tonight: what to look for in the sky, when the next launch is.
- A way to go deeper on one topic without hitting a 266-page archive.

## What blocks her

- Specialised words she doesn’t know.
- Articles that assume the user works in the field.
- Search and category pages that don’t really help.
- No clear direction towards what to read first.

## Needs mapped to design decisions

1. A gist box before the article body.
2. Reading time on every card. 
3. Category chips on cards plus working filter and search.
4. Related links at the end of an article.
5. A start-here entry point per category that continues into a sequence: a learning path where each article points to the next one.
6. Phone-first layout, images with captions and credit lines.

## What this persona rules out

A live news feed, comments, a telescope shop, an infinite archive, an account she must create before reading anything. She wants a small number of good, understandable articles.
